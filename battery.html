FOUNDATION · INSIDE A LITHIUM-ION CELL

How a battery works

Where does a battery’s energy come from, and what changes when you charge it?

The idea

A lithium-ion cell has two hosts for lithium. Lithium is held more loosely in graphite than in cobalt oxide, so letting it move from one to the other releases energy. The catch is that the ion and its electron have to travel separately: the ion through the liquid inside the cell, the electron out through your device.

What you’re looking at

A cell cut open. On the left, graphite: stacked carbon sheets that hold lithium between them. On the right, grains of cobalt oxide. Between them sits a porous separator soaked in electrolyte, and above runs the wire through the load. Mint beads are lithium ions; violet beads are electrons.

Try it

Switch between Discharge and Charge to reverse both journeys. The slider sets how much lithium is in the graphite, and the graph and reactions below follow it. Inside graphite zooms in to the carbon sheets. The cell is idealised: it shows directions and amounts of lithium, not voltages or speeds.

Sources, geometry & model assumptions+

One consistent reaction state

This ideal insertion cell transfers lithium reversibly between equal amounts of C6 and CoO2 host units. Discharge changes graphite from Li0.60C6 to Li0.30C6 and cobalt oxide from Li0.60CoO2 to Li0.90CoO2. Charge retraces that partial window. These are illustrative compositions, not a full state-of-charge scale.

Switching modes preserves the current composition. Direction markers move continuously while composition stays fixed; the slider alone changes the lithium inventory. Electrode polarity stays fixed; electrochemical roles reverse: oxidation defines the anode, reduction defines the cathode. Battery engineering often names electrodes by their discharge roles; this visualization labels the active reaction explicitly.

The electrolyte has no net lithium accumulation in this bookkeeping model. Counterions, solvent, SEI, gradients, charge layers and side reactions are omitted. No voltage, current, physical rate or performance is calculated. The 3D cell is a cutaway diagram, not a manufactured cell design.

Inside graphite

Carbon atoms form hexagonal sheets. Lithium can occupy the galleries between them, entering or leaving through exposed edges. The aligned carbon sheets illustrate a local intercalated graphite motif. Real graphite changes layer registry and staging with composition; those structural changes are not solved here.

Site brightness encodes fractional mean occupancy from the same x as the graph. The highlighted moving sphere explains the direction through a gallery; it is not an additional counted atom. Sizes and spacing are selected for legibility. The motion is illustrative, not molecular dynamics or a computed minimum-energy path.
