FOUNDATION · DIFFUSION

Diffusion in a particle

Why do smaller particles fill with lithium so much faster?

The idea

A battery electrode is a powder of tiny particles. Lithium can only get into each one through its surface, and then has to spread inward by diffusion: random hopping, with no push toward the center. How long that takes is one of the limits on how fast the material can charge.

What you’re looking at

A round particle with a quarter cut away, so you can see inside. The cut faces are colored by how much lithium is there, from dark purple for empty to blue for full, with contour lines at 20, 40, 60 and 80 percent. The beads show where the lithium is: they follow the amount, not the random path of any one ion. The film loops: lithium goes in, then comes back out.

Try it

Below the film, change the particle’s radius and compare how far lithium has spread after the same 2 seconds. Diffusion time grows with the square of the distance, so half the radius takes a quarter of the time. This is an ideal sphere with one fixed diffusivity, not a measurement of a real material.

MODEL NOTES

A smaller distance
A different timescale

The diagram uses the analytical series solution for diffusion into a sphere (Crank, The Mathematics of Diffusion, 2nd ed., 1975, eq. 6.18). The surface concentration is held constant; concentration starts at zero throughout the interior. Diffusivity is constant and the sphere does not change shape.

The concentration profile is shown at the same illustrative elapsed time and diffusivity for each radius. The characteristic diffusion time scales as R² / D. Doubling the radius gives four times the characteristic time at fixed D.

c(x, τ) = 1 − 2/(πx) Σ [ (−1)ⁿ⁺¹ sin(nπx) / n ] e−n²π²τ

Here x = r/R and τ = Dt/R². The preview uses D = 10⁻¹⁶ m²/s, t = 2 s, and 40 series terms. These are illustrative inputs, not measured properties of a particular cathode.

The film at the top uses the same equation, solved numerically while the surface concentration switches between 1 and 0 every 8 seconds of film. It shows the repeating cycle, so the last frame joins the first. The colors and contours on the cut faces come straight from that solution. Each bead is placed so the beads on a face follow the same concentration, which makes them trace the flow of lithium rather than individual random walks. The cutaway is a viewing aid, not a change to the sphere’s boundary conditions. The film’s time readout uses the radius chosen below.

The model omits particle interactions, electrode reactions, changing diffusivity, phase transitions, and electrochemical boundary conditions. It cannot predict a real battery’s capacity or charging speed.
